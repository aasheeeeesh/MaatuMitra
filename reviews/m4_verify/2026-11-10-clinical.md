## Findings

### CRITICAL
- **M4-C1 C4 applies an ADULT range to every patient.** No field says whether the patient is a child, and C4 does not look at the encounter. "ಮಗುವಿಗೆ paracetamol 1000 mg ... ದಿನಕ್ಕೆ ಮೂರು ಬಾರಿ" (paracetamol 1000 mg three times a day, for a child) PASSES C4: 1000 mg is in range and 3000 mg is not above the max. U3: children are dosed by age/weight, and 500 mg tablets start only at age 10. Fix: when the transcript or encounter indicates a child (for example ಮಗು / ಮಗುವಿಗೆ, or a caregiver speaking in an ASHA visit) or the record carries an age below 12, C4 must return `flag` "paediatric dose: weight-based, adult range not applicable" (fail-closed). Long-term: add age/weight to record.v2.

### MAJOR
- **M4-M1 Blank-range policy (`blank_range_fails_closed: true`)**: as a SAFETY default it is sensible. An unverifiable dose must never be shown as "pass". The problem is the measurement. 12 lexicon drugs have no range, and every count unit (tablet, capsule, sachet, ml) also hits the "no verified range for unit" branch. As a result, a large share of CORRECT gold doses are flagged: every "ಒಂದು ಮಾತ್ರೆ" (one tablet) dose, and every mg dose of zinc, clindamycin, the steroids, salbutamol, diclofenac and montelukast. The flag rate and false-positive rate then partly measure lexicon gaps, not verifier skill. Recommendation: keep fail-closed, but emit a DISTINCT reason code (for example `unverifiable:no_range`). Report it separately from `out_of_range` in E3/E4 tables. For count units, convert to mg when the drug has exactly one strength, or allow 1-2 tablets/capsules per dose.
- **M4-M2 Daily-total and regimen checks are weak.** The max-daily check runs only when `max_daily_mg` is set, and it is blank for 25 of 34 drugs. Example: levofloxacin 750 mg x 4/day (3000 mg/day) passes C4, as does azithromycin 2000 mg x 1/day x 5 days. Duration is never checked for plausibility. Suggest adding max_daily from labels (ciprofloxacin 1500 mg/day = 750 mg q12h, U9; the others UNVERIFIED) and a per-drug allowed frequency set (for example levofloxacin 1/day).
- **M4-M3 Allowed units are global.** `allowed_units` permits ml/teaspoon/sachet/tablet for every drug, so "ceftriaxone 1 tablet" or "ORS 500 mg" pass the unit test. There is no route check. Set `allowed_units_by_drug`: ceftriaxone and cefazolin [mg, g] (injection); ors [sachet, ml]; zinc [mg, tablet]; iron-folic acid [tablet, mg, mcg].
- **M4-M4 E4 drug swap (`_swap_drug`)**: (a) at record level, the swap may pick confusables that are NOT in the lexicon (prednisone, fomepizole, sertraline, sulfasalazine, folinic acid). C2/C4 then catch them trivially with "not in lexicon", which inflates detection. Report those separately or require `need_in_lexicon=True` at both levels. (b) For the 12 drugs with no confusable, the swap falls back to a random lexicon drug, which is not a realistic confusion. Report fallback swaps as their own row. (c) Add the missing clinically important within-lexicon pairs (dexamethasone<->prednisolone, diclofenac<->ibuprofen; AI-proposed, UNVERIFIED).
- **M4-M5 Severity taxonomy (docs/severity.md) misses clinically critical classes.** (1) ROUTE (oral vs injection). (2) PATIENT POPULATION / weight-based paediatric dosing (see M4-C1). (3) DOSE FORM / STRENGTH, for example syrup 120 mg/5 ml vs 250 mg/5 ml: "5 ml" is correct in both, but the mg delivered doubles. CR-DOSE covers unit mismatches but not concentration. (4) ADHERENCE (stopped early vs never taken; D-62). (5) AS-NEEDED/conditional instructions ("only if fever") recorded as a fixed frequency. Recommend adding CR-ROUTE, CR-POPULATION and CR-STRENGTH (critical) and MA-ADHERENCE and MA-PRN (major) with a decision entry. Changing the taxonomy after experiments start must be reported (severity.md header). The record.v1 schema has no route, form, age or as-needed field, so these need a contract change.

### MINOR
- **M4-m1 C5 phrase coverage**: the Indian prescription shorthand "1-0-1" (morning-noon-night), "every 8 hours"/"q8h"/"ಎಂಟು ಗಂಟೆಗೆ ಒಮ್ಮೆ", "HS"/night-only, and "SOS"/PRN do not parse. Fail-closed makes these flags, which is safe, but they become false alarms on real prescriptions. Also, `per` is set to "day" if ANY per-day word occurs anywhere in the span, so a mixed span can be mislabelled.
- **M4-m2 The "unusual: above typical strengths" soft note** is useful. Keep it a note, not a flag.
- **M4-m3 E4 frequency list includes 6/day.** It is fine as a planted error, but it makes record-level type g partly detectable by plausibility rather than by the span. State this in the E4 report.

## Is "blank range => flag" sensible? (requested judgement)
Yes for safety, no for measurement as currently reported. A verifier must not certify a dose it cannot check. But the flag should say "cannot verify" (and be counted separately), not imply the dose is wrong. Otherwise clinicians learn to ignore C4 flags (alarm fatigue), and E3/E4 precision is confounded by lexicon gaps.

## Recommended decisions (for the orchestrator; reviewers do not write handoff files)
1. Child-aware C4 (M4-C1) before any reported E4/E-run.
2. Split C4 reasons into `out_of_range`, `exceeds_max_daily`, `unverifiable_no_range`, `unit_not_allowed`.
3. Taxonomy additions (M4-M5) via a new decision entry, reported as a post-registration change if experiments have started.
